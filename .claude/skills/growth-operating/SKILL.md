# Growth Operating

The tools do not decide anything. Two gates do: **is this creator worth building for**, and **is this offer worth building**. Run the gates before spending outreach or build time.

## Pipeline and tool handoffs

| Stage | What happens | Tool |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Source | Find creators in a niche with buying intent | ListKit (CRM) |
| 2. Vet | Score the creator with the gate below. Skip fails. | This skill |
| 3. Outreach | Message that opens a conversation, not a pitch | Ghostwriter OS |
| 4. Call and close | Agree terms in writing before any build | Deal terms checklist below |
| 5. Offer design | Turn the creator's knowledge into an offer | This skill, then Synthesise AI for the product outline |
| 6. Payment setup | Revenue split configured before launch | Whop revenue split |
| 7. Launch assets | Sales page, emails, posts, scripts | Ghostwriter OS |

Never move a creator from stage 2 to 3 without a written vetting memo. Never move from stage 4 to 5 without signed terms.

## Sourcing method: the productised peer

Search only surfaces creators who already sell, because selling is what
generates the pages a search index can find. That looks like a dead end for
finding creators with nothing built. It is not, if you invert it.

1. Find a creator in the niche who **has** productised successfully.
2. Treat them as market research, not as a prospect. Their existing product is
   proof that this audience pays for this outcome, evidenced by a competitor
   rather than by anybody's opinion.
3. Then find their **peers**: people doing the same work, for the same kind of
   audience, who have not packaged it.

The productised creator validates the market. Their un-productised peer is the
partner. This is the only demand evidence available without reading comments,
and it is stronger than a self-reported score because somebody is actually
charging for it.

## Gate 1: creator vetting

Score each line 0, 1, or 2. Record evidence for every score, not a feeling.

| Signal | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audience trust | Followers only, no replies or DMs | Some replies, occasional questions | Audience asks the creator for advice unprompted |
| Buying intent in niche | Entertainment niche, no history of paying | Niche has paid products but creator has never sold | Niche pays and the creator's audience already asks what to buy |
| Engagement quality | Likes only | Comments are generic | Comments show specific problems the audience wants solved |
| Existing monetisation | Nothing sold, no list | Affiliate or sponsor deals only | Has sold their own product or has an email list |
| Responsiveness | Slow or ghosts | Replies within days | Replies within a day and asks questions back |
| Content consistency | Sporadic | Posts weekly | Posts several times a week for 6+ months |

**Pass: 8 or more, with no 0 in Audience trust or Buying intent.** A creator with a huge following and a 0 on either of those is a fail. Big audience does not mean buying audience.

Red flags that fail regardless of score: has partnered with another operator in the last 6 months and it ended badly, wants the product built before agreeing a split, or cannot name a specific problem their audience keeps asking about.

Output a vetting memo:

```
Creator:
Niche:
Score: X/12
Evidence per signal:
Red flags:
Decision: pitch / skip
The one problem their audience keeps asking about:
```

## Gate 2: offer design

Fill every line. A blank line means the offer is not ready for Synthesise AI.

1. **Problem.** One sentence, in the audience's words, taken from real comments or DMs. Not the creator's framing.
2. **Promise.** The specific outcome and the time it takes. "Land your first freelance client in 30 days," not "level up your freelancing."
3. **Proof.** Why this creator can promise it: their own result, or results they have already produced for others. No proof, no offer.
4. **Delivery format.** Pick the simplest format that delivers the promise: template pack, short course, cohort, community, coaching. Start small. A first offer that takes more than 2 weeks to build is too big.
5. **Price.** Anchor to the value of the outcome, not the hours in the product. State the price and the reason.
6. **Why now.** What makes the audience buy this month rather than bookmarking it.

Kill the offer if: the problem came from the creator rather than the audience, the promise cannot be checked by the buyer, or the creator has no proof.

Hand the completed six lines to Synthesise AI to produce the product outline. Review the outline against the promise: every module must move the buyer toward the promised outcome, or it gets cut.

## Deal terms checklist

Agree all of these in writing before building anything:

- Revenue split percentage and whether it is on gross or net of Whop fees
- Who pays for tools and ad spend
- Who owns the product, the sales page copy, and the customer list
- Refund policy and who absorbs refunds
- Minimum promotion commitment from the creator (posts per week, email sends)
- Term length and what happens to the product if either side leaves

Configure the Whop revenue split to match the signed terms before the launch date.

## Launch assets brief

When handing to Ghostwriter OS for stage 7, give it the six offer lines plus the vetting memo. Every asset must lead with the problem in the audience's words and close with the promise. Reject any asset that talks about the creator instead of the buyer.
