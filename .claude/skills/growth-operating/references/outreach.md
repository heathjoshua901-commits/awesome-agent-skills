# Outreach

Stage 3. How to open a conversation with a vetted creator.

## Precondition

**A written vetting memo exists for this creator.** No memo, no message. That is
the skill's standing rule and it exists because outreach written from a guess
reads like outreach written from a guess.

The memo supplies the two things the message needs: a verbatim comment showing
what their audience asks for, and the one problem that audience keeps raising.

## The principle: build before you ask

Ordinary growth-operator outreach asks for fifteen minutes to discuss a
partnership. It fails because it requests time before giving anything, and
because every creator with an audience receives several a week.

You can produce a complete, designed lead magnet for their specific audience in
about two minutes. So do that first, then lead with it.

**Give the finished thing. Ask for nothing but permission to send it.**

This works because it inverts what the creator expects. They are braced for a
pitch and receive a gift. It also proves capability in a way no claim can: the
work is already done and they can see it.

## Before sending, per creator

1. Run the creator through the platform analysis. Note the highest-rated theme.
2. Build a lead magnet against that theme, not against a generic niche.
3. **Read it end to end.** The generator leaks template markers and duplicate
   pull quotes into finished output. Sending an unread file destroys the exact
   credibility the asset was meant to build.
4. Fix the defects and export a clean PDF.

Two minutes to generate, ten minutes to check. The check is not optional.

## Route two: the creator already sells something

The principle holds but the asset changes. Do **not** build a competing product
or a lead magnet that duplicates what they teach. That reads as "your thing is
not good enough", which ends the conversation.

Build **the free step in front of their paid thing.** A short guide that makes
someone want the training they already sell, and that ends by pointing at it.
Additive, not competitive, and it demonstrates in one move that you understand
their business rather than just their content.

### Establish the baseline first

You cannot pitch a lift without knowing what it lifts from. Before writing:

| Find | Where |
|---|---|
| What they sell, exactly | Bio link, link tree, their site |
| The price | Checkout or sales page |
| How they sell it | Is there a free step, an email capture, a sequence, or only posts? |
| What it covers | Sales page curriculum or module list |

That last one decides what your free guide can say without overlapping.

The baseline is also the number your share gets measured against, so it is
commercial groundwork, not research.

### Check the free tier before you build, not after

Open their site and read the whole free layer first: guides and PDFs, webinars
live and on-demand, newsletter signup, podcast, free consultation. Building the
asset first and discovering the overlap afterwards wastes the build and would
have wasted the message.

Webinar and PDF titles are the highest-value thing on the page, because they name
the problems the creator already believes their audience has — and they are the
exact ground your asset must not re-cover. One screened coach ran a webinar
called "Why Being Fully Booked Still Isn't Making You More Profitable", which is
the pricing audit's whole argument under a different name.

### Do not send if

- They already have a lead magnet in front of the product. The observation
  collapses and you look like you did not check.
- You cannot name the price. You do not understand the business yet.
- Your guide covers the same ground as their paid product.

## Channel

| Channel | When |
|---|---|
| Instagram or TikTok DM | Default for creators under roughly 50,000 followers, where the creator still reads their own messages |
| Email | Whenever a business address is findable in the bio, a link tree, or a website. Higher chance of being read properly, lower chance of being seen at all |
| Both | Large accounts, where a DM is likely screened. Email first, DM as a nudge pointing at the email |

ListKit is for finding the email address and holding the record once you have a
name. It does not find creators.

## Message structure

Four parts, in this order, short enough to read without expanding.

1. **A specific observation.** Something only someone who watched their content
   could say. Name the post, the series, the speciality.
2. **Their audience's problem, in the audience's words.** Quote a real comment.
   This is the line that proves you are not sending a template.
3. **What you made.** One sentence on the asset and who it is for.
4. **A one-word ask.** "Want it?" or "Shall I send it over?" Nothing that
   requires scheduling.

No mention of partnership, revenue split, funnels, or a call. Those come after
they reply.

### Template

> Hi [name] — your [specific thing: the series on X, the breakdown of Y] is the
> reason I started watching.
>
> Something keeps coming up underneath those posts. [Verbatim comment], and
> variations of it several times over.
>
> I built a short guide answering exactly that, written for your audience rather
> than a general one. [Title]. It is finished and it is yours whether or not we
> ever speak again.
>
> Want me to send it over?

Adapt every line. A template sent as written is a template, and they can tell.

## Follow up once, never twice

One follow-up, four or five days later, and it must **add** something rather
than repeat the ask.

> Sent this over last week and I know the inbox is a lot. Attaching it anyway
> rather than making you reply for it. [Attach or link the PDF.]
>
> If it is useful, keep it. If not, no reply needed.

Then stop. A third message converts nobody and costs you the option of
approaching again in six months.

## When they reply

The reply is not the deal. It is permission to have a conversation.

Send the file. Ask one question about their audience that the analysis could not
answer, because that starts a discussion rather than a transaction. Only when
they ask what you do, or what you want, do you describe the arrangement.

Before any build work begins, the deal terms checklist in `SKILL.md` gets
completed in writing. Including the line about whether the split covers order
bumps and upsells.

## What fails

- Leading with the offer or the revenue split
- Asking for a call as the first ask
- Complimenting the creator with nothing specific attached
- Sending the same asset to several creators in a niche. It is two minutes each.
  Build one per creator or the gift is not a gift
- Sending an unread generated file

## Volume

This is deliberately slow. Each creator costs roughly fifteen minutes of real
work before a message is sent. Ten well-built approaches will outperform a
hundred templates, and the ten leave you with ten assets you own.
